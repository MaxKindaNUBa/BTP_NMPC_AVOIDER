Keywords:                                              This paper presents a path-following and collision avoidance system for autonomous surface vehicles based
Autonomous surface vehicle                             on nonlinear model predictive control. The proposed strategy is capable of following a desired path while
Nonlinear model predictive control                     maintaining a commanded velocity, whereas it can diverge from the references to safely perform maneuvers
Obstacle avoidance
                                                       to elude unexpected obstacles. A nonlinear dynamic model of the vehicle is applied to predict the vehicle states
                                                       into a finite horizon. Furthermore, a LiDAR sensor located at the front of the boat is employed for local object
                                                       detection. Here, the high-performance optimal control framework, acados, is used to solve the optimization
                                                       problem onboard. Real-time numerical simulations and field experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the
                                                       proposed approach against multiple buoys.

4.3. NMPC implementation

      Considering the high-performance embedded capability, the aca-
dos optimal control framework (Verschueren et al., 2018) is selected
to solve the NLP (22). The acados framework has been tested with
practical systems such as quadrotors (Carlos et al., 2020) and scale
racing cars (Kloeser et al., 2020). Additionally, acados is faster than its
predecessor, ACADO (Verschueren et al., 2018). A Real-Time Iteration                             Fig. 5. Simulation I. Speed reference and response (top). Thruster signals (bottom).

scheme with Sequential Quadratic Programming and Gauss–Newton
Hessian approximation approximately solves the NLP (Carlos et al.,
2020). The acados framework outputs embedded C-code which is then
implemented as part of a C++ ROS node to be integrated with the ASV
software via rostopics.
      For experimental evaluation, the NMPC time horizon was set to
 = 4 s, with  = 400. Since the LiDAR can run at a frequency
of 20 Hz, the frequency of the ROS node was also set to 20 Hz, and
the value of port , stbd was taken at  = 20. Furthermore, the safety
distance was chosen as  = 0.2 m, and the constraints were selected
as min = −1.5, max = 1.5, min = −5.0, max = 5.0, ̇ R,max = −90, and
̇ F,max = 90. The weights were selected experimentally as
